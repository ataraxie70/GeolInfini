# 1. Purpose

This document defines the knowledge model of Project Atlas.

It establishes the concepts, principles, lifecycle and relationships governing knowledge as the primary strategic asset of the enterprise.

The model provides the foundation for Knowledge Architecture, Business Architecture, Data Architecture and future intelligent capabilities.

---

# 2. Knowledge Philosophy

Project Atlas considers knowledge to be its primary enterprise asset.

Products, services, applications and commercial activities derive their value from the enterprise's ability to acquire, structure, validate, preserve, enrich and apply knowledge.

Knowledge is therefore an enterprise capability rather than a by-product.

---

# 3. Enterprise Knowledge Lifecycle

Enterprise knowledge evolves through a continuous transformation process.

```text
Reality
    ↓
Observation
    ↓
Data
    ↓
Information
    ↓
Knowledge
    ↓
Understanding
    ↓
Decision
    ↓
Experience
    ↓
Validation
    ↓
Enterprise Knowledge
```

Each cycle strengthens the enterprise's ability to support future decisions.

---

# 4. Knowledge Categories

Project Atlas recognizes multiple complementary categories of knowledge.

## Technical Knowledge

Knowledge describing technologies, products, components, standards and specifications.

## Decision Knowledge

Knowledge describing how recommendations are produced under specific contexts and constraints.

## Community Knowledge

Knowledge emerging from discussions, questions, answers and collaborative exchanges.

## Experience Knowledge

Knowledge acquired through real-world product usage, maintenance and long-term feedback.

## Educational Knowledge

Knowledge intended to improve digital literacy and user understanding.

## Operational Knowledge

Knowledge supporting enterprise operations and service delivery.

## Lifecycle Knowledge

Knowledge describing the acquisition, maintenance, evolution and replacement of computing equipment.

## Context Knowledge

Knowledge describing local realities that influence decision quality, including market conditions, repairability, infrastructure and availability.

---

# 5. Knowledge Asset

A Knowledge Asset is any reusable unit of validated knowledge maintained by the enterprise.

Knowledge Assets include, but are not limited to:

* articles;
* videos;
* buying guides;
* tutorials;
* technical sheets;
* benchmark analyses;
* product documentation;
* comparison studies;
* FAQs;
* experience reports;
* curated discussion summaries.

Knowledge Assets may reference one another and shall never exist in isolation.

---

# 6. Enterprise Knowledge Fabric

The Enterprise Knowledge Fabric is the conceptual layer that connects all enterprise knowledge, interactions and decisions.

It links:

* Knowledge Assets;
* Products;
* Technologies;
* Community Interactions;
* Decision Models;
* Services;
* Customer Decision History;
* Lifecycle Events.

The Knowledge Fabric enables continuous knowledge circulation and enrichment throughout the enterprise.

---

# 7. Knowledge Genome

The Knowledge Genome represents the enduring body of rules, principles and relationships that define the identity of Project Atlas.

It includes:

* Enterprise Principles;
* Decision Rules;
* Context Rules;
* Validation Rules;
* Governance Rules;
* Lifecycle Rules;
* Community Rules;
* Recommendation Rules.

The Knowledge Genome evolves through governance while preserving enterprise identity.

---

# 8. Knowledge Governance

Enterprise knowledge shall be:

* validated;
* versioned;
* traceable;
* interconnected;
* reusable;
* continuously enriched.

Knowledge governance ensures long-term quality, consistency and preservation.

---

# 9. Knowledge Architecture

Knowledge Architecture is the architectural discipline responsible for designing how enterprise knowledge is created, structured, connected, governed, preserved and applied.

It complements the Business, Data, Application and Technology Architectures.

---

# 10. Strategic Implications

Knowledge is the foundation upon which every enterprise capability is built.

Every publication, discussion, recommendation, customer interaction and operational activity contributes to the continuous evolution of the enterprise knowledge base.

The long-term competitive advantage of Project Atlas resides in its ability to transform distributed information into trusted, explainable and reusable enterprise knowledge.
