# 1. Purpose

This document defines the fundamental problem that justifies the creation of Project Atlas.

Its objective is not to describe a solution, but to establish a shared understanding of the challenges faced by individuals and organizations when making decisions related to computing equipment and digital technologies.

Every architectural, business and technical decision throughout the project lifecycle shall contribute to addressing the problems identified in this document.

---

# 2. Context

Computing technologies have become essential to education, employment, entrepreneurship, administration, research and everyday life.

At the same time, the computing market has experienced continuous growth in the diversity of available hardware, software and digital services.

While this diversity increases opportunities, it also significantly increases the complexity of decision making.

Choosing an appropriate computing solution has become a knowledge-intensive activity requiring technical expertise that most users do not possess.

---

# 3. Core Problem

The computing market is primarily organized around products rather than user needs.

Consumers are generally expected to identify technical specifications, compare hardware components, evaluate compatibility, understand performance metrics and anticipate future requirements before making purchasing decisions.

This expectation creates a structural mismatch between the complexity of technology and the knowledge available to most users.

As a consequence, purchasing decisions frequently become uncertain, stressful and inefficient.

---

# 4. Identified Challenges

The following challenges characterize the current ecosystem.

## 4.1 Information Overload

Users are exposed to an overwhelming quantity of technical information without sufficient contextualization.

Specifications are abundant, but explanations are often inadequate for non-specialists.

---

## 4.2 Fragmented Knowledge

Relevant information is distributed across numerous independent sources.

Users must navigate multiple websites, forums, videos, reviews and vendor catalogs before forming a complete understanding of available options.

---

## 4.3 Technology-Centered Purchasing

Current purchasing processes generally begin with products.

Users are invited to browse catalogs before clearly identifying their own requirements.

The purchasing journey is therefore driven by available inventory rather than actual needs.

---

## 4.4 Rapid Technological Evolution

Hardware generations evolve rapidly.

Performance standards, compatibility requirements and software demands continuously change, making previous knowledge quickly obsolete.

Users struggle to maintain an accurate understanding of the market.

---

## 4.5 Lack of Long-Term Guidance

Most commercial interactions end once a purchase has been completed.

Limited support is provided regarding maintenance, upgrades, lifecycle planning or future equipment evolution.

---

## 4.6 Trust Deficit

Users often question whether recommendations are genuinely aligned with their interests or primarily motivated by commercial objectives.

The absence of transparent decision support reduces confidence throughout the purchasing process.

---

# 5. Impact

These challenges generate significant consequences for multiple stakeholders.

Individuals may purchase equipment that is unsuitable, unnecessarily expensive or incapable of supporting future needs.

Educational institutions and organizations may invest inefficiently in digital infrastructure.

Professionals may experience productivity losses resulting from inadequate technology choices.

Vendors must dedicate considerable effort to correcting purchasing mistakes that could have been avoided through better guidance.

---

# 6. Root Causes

The identified problems originate from several structural factors.

* Increasing technological complexity.
* Rapid innovation cycles.
* Fragmentation of information sources.
* Product-oriented commercial models.
* Limited accessibility of technical knowledge.
* Insufficient decision-support mechanisms.
* Weak continuity between purchasing and long-term equipment management.

---

# 7. Opportunity

The evolution of digital technologies creates an opportunity to rethink how people interact with computing ecosystems.

Rather than focusing exclusively on product distribution, future digital services can support the complete decision-making process by combining structured knowledge, guidance, transparency and long-term assistance.

This opportunity extends beyond electronic commerce and concerns the broader relationship between people and technology.

---

# 8. Architectural Implications

The problems identified in this document imply that the future enterprise shall be designed around user decision support rather than product distribution alone.

Business capabilities, information models, digital services and governance mechanisms shall prioritize understanding user needs before proposing technological solutions.

This principle shall guide all subsequent architecture activities.

---

# 9. Success Indicator

The problems described in this document will be considered effectively addressed when individuals and organizations can confidently make informed computing decisions based on their actual needs rather than their level of technical expertise.
